Results report October 2026 · NHC acyloin campaign

Why the GROVER catalyst encoding leads, what could replace it, and what a Breslow-intermediate encoding would need

cross_1 · 69 experiments · 22 catalysts tested · 161-catalyst library · primary and leave-one-out studies (60 + 60 folds) · monroe_m3 ten-setting screen (30 configurations)

Background

The reaction and the optimisation problem

We optimise yield of an NHC-catalysed acyloin condensation of formaldehyde with a Gaussian process (GP) over catalyst, solvent, base and four continuous conditions. In the crossed grid cross_1 (2,520 GP configurations, 137 folds each) the catalyst encoding grover_pca is the best of seven: median gem +0.284, 0.08 to 0.14 above the others, best configuration gem +0.425 (grover_pca catalyst, Hansen solvent descriptors, grover_pca base block, Matérn-5/2, probit transform, per-block lengthscale prior P2). GROVER is a 2020 model that independent benchmarks rank below ECFP, so its lead needs an explanation before it is relied on, and the newer encoders that could replace it need a cheap test.

The GROVER encoding

GROVER-large (Rong et al., NeurIPS 2020) is a graph transformer pretrained on 11 million ZINC15 and ChEMBL molecules to predict masked one-hop atom and bond contexts (2,518 atom and 2,686 bond classes) and 85 RDKit functional-group counts. We use it frozen in fingerprint mode on the pre-catalyst SMILES as stored, counter-ion included: four mean-pooled embedding sets of 1,200 columns each (atom-from-atom, atom-from-bond, bond-from-atom, bond-from-bond) followed by 200 RDKit 2D descriptors scaled to [0, 1], 5,000 columns, reduced by PCA over the 161-catalyst library to the 16 components at the variance knee (92.4 percent of the variance). A bond-less counter-ion such as \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\) enters the two atom-level means only.

How a configuration is scored

Primary metric and the gem

CRPS measures a predictive distribution against the observed yield in yield percentage points; the baseline quotes the training-fold mean and variance; CRPSS = 0 means no better than the training average, +0.4 means 40 percent lower probabilistic error. The skill score is pooled over every test point of a study:

\[ \mathrm{CRPSS} = 1 - \frac{\sum_i \mathrm{CRPS}_i(\text{model})}{\sum_i \mathrm{CRPS}_i(\text{baseline})}, \qquad \mathrm{gem} = \min(\mathrm{CRPSS}_{\text{primary}}, \mathrm{CRPSS}_{\text{LOO}}) \]

The gem is the smaller of the pooled CRPSS on primary and on LOO. primary is 10 \(\times\) 6-fold catalyst-grouped cross-validation (60 folds, about 51 training points per fold), evaluating generalisation across catalysts; leave-one-out (LOO) is 60 folds with 68 training points per fold, evaluating interpolation within the measured set.

Paired comparison

Every swap below changes only the catalyst block of the rank-1 configuration and reuses the stored run's fold seeds. Differences are paired fold bootstraps (4,000 resamples of the 60 folds), reported as median and 95 percent interval.

A single gem has a bootstrap standard deviation of 0.066; the paired standard deviation of a difference between two similar catalyst blocks is 0.03 to 0.05.

1. The appended RDKit descriptors play no part

Deng et al. 2023 and Sun et al. 2022 show the 200 RDKit descriptors carry GROVER's published benchmark gains, and a ridge model on those descriptors predicts the first five embedding PCs with \(R^2\) 0.80 to 0.95. One would expect removing them to degrade model skill.

RDKit 200 only (knee) 9 +0.178 -0.257 [-0.443, -0.066]
RDKit functional-group counts fr_* only (the motif target) 7 +0.073 -0.363 [-0.589, -0.140]

Removing the 200 descriptors changes the LOO CRPSS by less than 0.01; the descriptors alone lose 0.26.

After per-column standardisation the 200 descriptors are 4 percent of 5,000 columns and carry 3.2 percent of the variance of the 16 components; linear CKA between the full and the embedding-only reduction on the 22 tested catalysts is 0.99, and 18 of 21 screened catalysts keep the same nearest neighbour. The descriptors are predictable from the embedding, not the other way round: what the GP uses is the learned embedding.

2. The GP uses five components as a smoother over about five related catalysts

Refitting the rank-1 configuration on all 69 experiments with the production code yields lengthscales in min-max-scaled units: short on components 1, 2, 3, 4 and 9 (0.38 to 0.56), long on 0, 5, 6 and 10 to 15 (1.4 to 2.6). The P2 prior for this 16-column block has a median of 16.5 and a log-scale of 2.0, so the data pull every catalyst lengthscale well below it. At these lengthscales the Matérn-5/2 catalyst correlation between a tested catalyst and its nearest neighbour is 0.78 (median), with the other 21 it is 0.23 on average, and the sum of correlations (effective number of neighbours) is 4.6.

The skill is flat between 8 and 16 components and falls by 0.07 to 0.13 beyond; the variance knee happens to sit at the upper edge of the plateau.

Each extra component adds a lengthscale that 51 to 68 training points must switch off, and the P2 prior's location grows with the block width (\(\log\sqrt{D}\), Hvarfner et al. 2024). The knee is chosen without seeing any yield, so this is not selection on outcomes, but the lead depends on keeping the block short.

5. The pretrained embedding beats a substructure embedding with the same neighbour structure

mol2vec_pca (Morgan-substructure word2vec, 2018; 16 components) shares 14 of 21 nearest neighbours with grover_pca, linear CKA 0.88, and the same 1-NN screen-yield error (7.6 pp), yet scores LOO +0.353, \(\Delta\) -0.081 [-0.159, -0.018].

Substructure similarity explains most of the geometry but not all of the skill; the remaining 0.08 is the pretrained part.

6. The pre-catalyst graph carries most of the lead; the counter-ion a fifth of it

H2 asked whether the separate pre-catalyst forms of one carbene (\(\mathrm{CO}_2\) adduct, chloride, free carbene, bicarbonate, tetrafluoroborate) matter. We ran GROVER-large again on two other inputs (installation and versions in PROVENANCE.md): the free carbene of each catalyst, combined with the four-column pre-catalyst-form one-hot as the grid does for every _carbene level, and the pre-catalyst with its counter-ion removed. Both through the same 16-or-fewer-component PCA knee, at the rank-1 settings, primary and LOO.

Encoding the free carbene instead of the pre-catalyst costs 0.29 of gem even with the form one-hot; removing only the counter-ion costs 0.065.

The four-column one-hot does not replace what the embedding of the whole pre-catalyst carries; with the carbene input, catalysts 1 to 5 share one embedding point and differ only by the one-hot. The same drop appears for every other model in Part II (0.27 to 0.38), so it is a property of the input, not of GROVER. H2 is supported: the lead needs the pre-catalyst graph, and about a fifth of the loss from carbene input is the counter-ion.

7. A nearest-neighbour proxy can order encodings but cannot pre-filter them

Over the 50 catalyst encodings of the elimination run, Spearman correlation between a cheap proxy computed on the 21 screened catalysts and the elimination gem: LOO MAE of an isotropic GP on the mean screen yield -0.60 against the best gem per encoding (\(p \lt 0.001\)), -0.38 against the median; 1-NN error -0.39 / -0.16; 3-NN -0.11 / +0.04; effective rank of the encoding -0.20.

The isotropic-GP proxy is the only usable one, and it would have discarded graphormer_precat_pca, fifth in the elimination run (proxy 11.8 pp against 8.5 pp for grover_pca).

Therefore the cheapest reliable filter is a GP run itself (Part III).

Limitation

Everything above holds at one configuration (rank 1) and on 22 catalysts; the leave-one-scaffold-out study, where grover_pca is fourth (+0.071 against +0.158 for scage_carbene), is not addressed by these tests.

What the literature says

Independent frozen-embedding benchmarks place GROVER 17th of 25 (mean rank 17.1, ECFP 7.5; Praski et al. 2025); its published gains came from appended RDKit descriptors and optimistic splits (Deng et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2022). Those benchmarks score drug-like ADMET tasks with hundreds to tens of thousands of molecules and random-forest, logistic or kNN heads. Our setting differs in three ways: 22 congeneric catalysts, interpolation inside the measured set, and a GP whose ARD metric reweights a short PCA block. Deep embeddings do well when the target is smooth over structural neighbours (Xia et al. 2023), which is the situation the GP creates here.

Part III

A screening plan that swaps only the catalyst block

A full grid per candidate is out of reach; every option keeps the non-catalyst axes of the top cross_1 configurations and swaps the catalyst block, always with a grover_pca control rerun under current code. Costs from cross_1's measured fold times: 477 CPU-h for 2,520 cells (0.77 of the projection); one primary + LOO cell takes a median 7.5 CPU-min at 16 columns, 12.2 at 516 and 19.2 at 1,540; primary and LOO are 87.4 percent of all fold time.

Monroe is the one candidate that passed M2; its M3 screen is Part IV.

Part V

Breslow intermediates as the catalyst input

The proposal is to encode each catalyst by its Breslow intermediate with formaldehyde, the enaminol formed when the free carbene adds to formaldehyde, instead of the pre-catalyst or the free carbene. We built these SMILES for the whole library to see what the representation gives and what it still lacks; no model was run on them.

14. Writing the SMILES is easy and removes the ingestion problems

From the free carbene of each catalyst we attach =CH–OH at the carbene carbon (enol form), and also build the keto tautomer (C2–H, CH=O) and the zwitterionic tetrahedral adduct (\(\mathrm{C2}^+ \text{--} \mathrm{CH}_2 \text{--} \mathrm{O}^-\)).

Catalysts with two carbene centres (bis-NHCs 61 and 78) 2, need a rule (one arm or both)
Enols with an E/Z or other stereo choice 79 of 159 (66 with two stereoisomers, 13 with 4 to 12)

A Breslow intermediate is a neutral, single-fragment, closed-shell molecule, so every model in the scan can read it, including those that cannot read salts or divalent carbon.

This is the main practical gain: KPGT, SMI-TED, the interatomic potentials and every 3D model lose their ingestion failure modes.

15. What SMILES alone do not provide

The pre-catalyst disappears. Catalysts 1 to 5 (SIMes as \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) adduct, chloride, free carbene, bicarbonate and tetrafluoroborate) become one molecule, as do 6 and 16 and 8 and 13. Yet finding 3 shows that the encoding the GP uses most distinguishes counter-ions, and the screen yields of these forms differ (catalyst 1: 53 percent, 2: 29 percent, 3: 44 percent, 4: 51 percent). The activation step (base needed, \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) loss, counter-ion) must then enter separately: the four-column form one-hot the grid already appends to _carbene levels, plus a counter-ion descriptor, plus the existing base-active flag. At the rank-1 settings, every model loses 0.29 to 0.38 of gem when using the free carbene with the form one-hot in place of the pre-catalyst (findings 6 and 9). A Breslow encoding merges the forms in the same way (22 tested pre-catalysts become 16 intermediates, against 17 free carbenes), so it starts from the carbene's position. It can only do better if the structure of the intermediate carries yield information that the carbene does not.

The measured cost of dropping the pre-catalyst graph (0.29 to 0.38 of gem) is the hurdle a Breslow encoding has to clear.

One species must be chosen and named: enol or keto tautomer, or the tetrahedral adduct; which one controls the turnover is a mechanistic claim (Berkessel et al. characterise the keto tautomer and spiro-dioxolane resting states; Mayr and co-workers measure nucleophilicities of Breslow-type enamines). With formaldehyde the second carbonyl addition and the formoin side chemistry also matter.

Stereochemistry: 79 enols have an E/Z choice that string and graph models with bond stereo read differently; it must be fixed (lowest-energy isomer from a conformer search) or both isomers averaged.

Geometry for the 3D models (Uni-Mol2, Frad, Transformer-M, SCAGE, SOAP, Monroe, the interatomic potentials): an RDKit conformer exists for 158 of 159, but a physically meaningful one, with the N-aryl twist and the enol O–H orientation, needs a GFN2-xTB conformer search, which the interpretable pipeline already runs for carbenes.

Out-of-distribution input: hydroxy ketene aminals are rare in the drug-like corpora of every pretrained model; the encodings will reflect general enamine chemistry more than Breslow-intermediate stability.

The physically motivated route is descriptors of the intermediate itself: xTB free energy of formation from carbene and formaldehyde, keto–enol free energy, HOMO energy of the enol as a nucleophilicity proxy. These are computed with the existing xTB pipeline and are interpretable, unlike an embedding.

Build _breslow levels for the 2D models first (minutes each) and score them with M2 in two forms: the intermediate with the form one-hot (directly comparable to the _carbene rows above), and the intermediate's PCA components concatenated with the pre-catalyst grover_pca block, which keeps the pre-catalyst information and asks whether the intermediate adds to it. Run the xTB descriptor set for the intermediates on Mechthild in parallel. A Breslow block that only matches the carbene rows adds nothing; a concatenated block that beats grover_pca is evidence that the intermediate carries signal the pre-catalyst graph does not.
