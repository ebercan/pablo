Setup

We optimise the yield of an NHC-catalysed acyloin condensation over the catalyst, solvent and base and four continuous conditions: temperature, formaldehyde ratio, catalyst loading and time. The surrogate is a Gaussian process. Its accuracy on experiments it has not seen depends on how the discrete choices are encoded, how the yield is transformed and how the lengthscales are regularised.

This grid crosses the seven catalyst encodings kept by the elimination run with every solvent and base encoding, two base kernels, three outcome transforms and two lengthscale priors. All 2,520 combinations are scored on the same 137 folds. The grid answers two questions: which choices carry the predictive skill, and how far apart the best configurations really are.

The _pca encodings are reduced to the principal components up to the variance knee. KT, Hansen and Gutmann are Kamlet–Taft, Hansen and Gutmann solvent descriptors. thermo and steric are proton-affinity and Sterimol base descriptors, and OHE is the one-hot identity control. Every column has its own lengthscale under both priors, and the design matrices are 21 to 1,568 columns wide. The kernel structure (KS1 product kernel), the constant mean and the single-task model (st_y) are fixed, so every result here holds for the product kernel only.

How a configuration is scored

For each held-out experiment the GP returns a predictive distribution F, not a single number. The continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) compares this distribution with the observed yield y:

CRPS(F,y)=E|X−y|−12E|X−X′|,X,X′∼Findependent

The first term is the expected distance between a draw from the forecast and the observation. The second term gives back the forecast's own spread. CRPS is measured in yield percentage points (pp), and lower is better. For a point forecast μ the second term vanishes and CRPS = |μ − y|. Averaged over test points, CRPS is the mean absolute error generalised to a distribution: it rewards a mean close to the observation and a spread that matches the size of the error. For a Gaussian forecast N(μ, σ²) it has a closed form:

CRPS=σ[z(2Φ(z)−1)+2φ(z)−1π],z=y−μσ

A large miss scores about |μ − y| − σ/√π, which is the absolute error minus a small credit for having admitted uncertainty. For a calibrated forecast, whose errors really are N(0, σ²), the ratio of CRPS to MAE is fixed:

E[CRPS]=σπ,E|y−μ|=σ2π⇒CRPSMAE=12≈0.71

CRPS is a proper score, so an interval that is too narrow or too wide raises this ratio. Across the grid the median CRPS/MAE is 0.71 on primary and 0.75 on leave-one-out: the primary intervals are close to calibrated and the leave-one-out intervals slightly too narrow (finding 9). Under probit and arcsin_sqrt the back-transformed forecast is not Gaussian, and CRPS is computed from samples on the yield scale. Every CRPS and MAE in this report is on the yield scale, so all transforms are compared on the same footing.

Within a study, CRPS is pooled over every test point of every fold and compared with a baseline that quotes the training-fold mean and variance for every test point. The gem takes the worse of the two interpolation studies:

CRPSS=1−∑iCRPSi(model)∑iCRPSi(baseline) gem=min(CRPSSprimary,CRPSSLOO)

CRPSS = 0 means the model is worth nothing over quoting the training average, and +0.4 means its probabilistic error is 40% smaller. A gem of +0.1 therefore means at least 10% better than the baseline on both interpolation studies.

Part IThe grid: the catalyst encoding carries the skill

All 2,520 configurations were fitted on all 137 folds, and none of the 345,240 fits failed. 2,139 configurations (85%) beat the baseline by at least 10% on both interpolation studies; 62 (2%) fall below it.

The figure shows where the spread in gem comes from. The medians of the catalyst encodings span 0.138, which is more than the transforms (0.101), the base encodings (0.076), the solvent encodings (0.061), the priors (0.046) or the kernels (0.025).

2A bounded transform and the per-block prior each add skill; raw yield is the weakest choice

Yield is a fraction in [0, 1] with many zeros and few high values, so a Gaussian likelihood on the raw fraction should fit worst, and it does. Moving from raw to probit raises the gem by a median of 0.094 in matched settings, more than any solvent or base choice. probit and arcsin_sqrt are within 0.022 of each other and share the top of the ranking. P2 beats P1 by 0.037 in 87% of matched settings, and the two effects add up: the median gem is +0.102 for raw with P1 and +0.246 for probit with P2. matern52 beats rbf by 0.022 and wins 79% of matched settings.

The prior effect comes from the Hvarfner location. P1 centres every lengthscale on log√D of the whole design matrix, so the 4-column continuous block gets the same location as a 1,568-column unimol block. P2 sets D per block. The gain from P2 grows with the width of the catalyst block: +0.096 for unimol and +0.087 for scage_carbene, against +0.017 for the 16-column minimol_precat_pca.

3Solvent descriptors beat the one-hot control; for the base, one-hot is as good as any descriptor

The 69 experiments contain eight solvents and six base labels, so a descriptor has little room to generalise and we expected one-hot to be hard to beat. For the solvent it is the worst choice: the two-column Gutmann block (donor number and permittivity) leads in 50% of matched settings, and one-hot is 0.059 below it. Both effects are a third to a half of the transform effect. Rank 1 uses Hansen and grover_pca, neither of which leads its axis: at the top of the ranking the solvent and base choices are inside the noise (finding 4).

4The top 74 configurations cannot be told apart

The gem rests on 60 + 60 folds of 69 experiments, so it carries sampling noise. We resample the folds of primary and leave-one-out with replacement 2,000 times, using the same draws for every configuration, and recompute every gem. The median bootstrap SD of a gem is 0.066.

For 74 configurations the 95% interval of the gem difference to rank 1 includes zero, and rank 1 has the highest gem in only 8% of draws. The most frequent bootstrap winner is rank 11 (32%). Of the 74 tied configurations, 54 use grover_pca, 9 minimol_precat_pca, 5 unimol, 4 soap_pca, and one each transformerm_precat_pca and interpretable_steric. None uses raw, and 51 use P2.

Rank 1 is separated from the best configuration of five of the other six encodings. The exception is minimol_precat_pca, whose best configuration (+0.318) is not separated.

The gem of rank 1 is optimistic

Rank 1 is the maximum of 2,520 noisy gems, so its gem is biased upward. The bootstrap resamples the same folds and does not remove this bias, so the expected out-of-sample skill of the selected configuration is below +0.425. The result we can defend is the family, not the winner: grover_pca (or minimol_precat_pca) with a bounded transform and, preferably, P2 reaches a gem near +0.40. 69 experiments do not resolve the solvent, base and kernel choices within that family.

6Ranked by MAE or R² alone, grover_pca still leads; the MAE ranking separates the top least

The gem scores the whole predictive distribution. If only the predictive mean matters, a point-error metric is the natural ranking, and a configuration with good means but badly sized intervals could move up. We build two more rankings in the same way as the gem, pooled over every test point and taking the worse of the two studies:

MAE score=max(MAEprimary,MAELOO) R2 score=min(Rprimary2,RLOO2) R2=1−∑(y−μ)2∑(y−y¯)2

MAE is measured on the predictive mean, in pp. R² is the classical coefficient of determination over the pooled test points of a study (0.561 for rank 1 on leave-one-out). It is not the per-fold median Q² of the other tables, which is meaningless on leave-one-out. The bootstrap reuses the 2,000 fold draws of finding 4.

Part IIIWhy grover_pca leads

GROVER is a graph transformer pretrained on 11 million drug-like molecules from ZINC15 and ChEMBL. Its pretraining tasks predict masked one-hop atom and bond contexts and 85 RDKit functional-group counts.

We use it frozen: the four mean-pooled embedding sets (4,800 columns) and 200 appended RDKit 2D descriptors of the pre-catalyst as stored, counter-ion included, reduced by PCA over the 161-catalyst library to 16 components. In independent frozen-embedding benchmarks GROVER ranks below ECFP on average, and much of its published advantage came from the appended descriptors. Nothing in its design points at NHC organocatalysis, so its lead here needs an explanation. We look for it in the stored predictions of the grid; no model was refitted for this part.

12The whole gain comes from the 43 screening experiments, where only the catalyst and the base vary

We compare grover_pca with each of the six other encodings in every one of the 360 matched settings. For each test experiment we record how much lower its CRPS is: the gain, in pp, where positive favours grover_pca. In leave-one-out each experiment is scored once per configuration; in primary its CRPS is averaged over the 10 repeats. The gain per experiment is the mean over the 2,160 matched pairs. 43 of the 69 experiments are the catalyst screen (THF, 80 °C, 2 eq formaldehyde, loading 5.25) over 21 catalysts; the other 26 vary the conditions on 7 catalysts.

On primary the screen carries all of the grover_pca gain, and on leave-one-out 89% of it. On the 26 condition-variation experiments the encodings are level. At the screening condition the four continuous columns are identical. The GP therefore predicts a held-out screen experiment from the screen yields of other catalysts, weighted by the similarity of their catalyst and base blocks, and the catalyst encoding becomes the whole model. The condition variations use catalysts 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 12 and 13, with 2 to 10 experiments each, so in leave-one-out a held-out variation usually has a sibling on the same catalyst in the training fold.

The largest gains are on the high-yield screen experiments of the SIMes catalysts 1 and 4 without base. 13grover_pca gives the most informative nearest neighbour among the screened catalysts, but the property is fragile

The GP compares catalysts through ARD lengthscales; here we use an unweighted proxy that needs no fitted model. We scale each catalyst block to [0, 1] over the screened catalysts, as the model's input normalisation does, and predict each catalyst's mean screen yield from its nearest neighbour or from its three nearest neighbours. unimol and scage_carbene are computed on the free carbene and include the four-column form one-hot they carry in the grid; soap_pca and interpretable_steric carry no form information. Predicting each catalyst with the mean of the other catalysts gives an error of 13.9 pp.

The nearest neighbour of a screened catalyst in grover_pca predicts its screen yield to 7.6 pp, the best of the seven encodings, while its three-neighbour prediction is among the worst. Its nearest-neighbour pairs are chemically close: 1 and 4 (SIMes CO₂ adduct and bicarbonate), 3 and 1, 6 and 7 (IMes CO₂ adducts), 9 and 10 (N,N′-diaryl imidazolinium chlorides), 11 and 12 (N,N′-dialkyl), 15 and 17 (benzothiazolium), 8 and 13 (SIPr), 18 and 20 (triazolium). Within each pair the screen yields differ by 9 pp or less. A GP with short lengthscales on the catalyst block behaves like such a local predictor, which fits finding 12.

The property is fragile. Recomputing the PCA of the 5,000 GROVER columns with 8, 12, 24 or 32 components instead of the knee's 16 gives nearest-neighbour errors of 9.9, 8.8, 10.9 and 12.5 pp, and 16 components of the embedding columns alone give 11.7 pp. The knee is set without access to any yield, so this is not selection on outcomes, but the lead may depend on the reduction as much as on the model.

14Four hypotheses, and the grid levels that decide them

H1 · Local similarity on the screen supported

grover_pca places each screened catalyst next to the catalyst that shares its scaffold and N-substituent class, and those pairs have similar screen yields. GROVER's one-hop context and functional-group pretraining targets encode ring type, N-aryl against N-alkyl substituents and counter-ion, which are the features along which the 22 catalysts differ. Findings 12 and 13 support this.

H2 · Distinct forms of one core kept close partly supported

Catalysts 1–5 share the SIMes carbene and differ as CO₂ adduct, free carbene, bicarbonate, chloride and tetrafluoroborate. soap_pca and interpretable_steric put all five on one point, and unimol and scage_carbene separate them only by the form one-hot. grover_pca keeps them distinct at a mean mutual distance of 0.37 of the mean pairwise distance, closer than minimol_precat_pca (0.47) and transformerm_precat_pca (0.51). On primary the gain on catalysts 1–5 is larger against the two encodings that merge them (+1.13, +0.98 pp) than against the two other pre-catalyst graphs (+0.13, +0.11 pp). On leave-one-out it is not (+0.51, +0.32 against +0.57, +1.41 pp).

H3 · The appended RDKit descriptors open

The 200 RDKit 2D descriptors carry 3.2% of the variance of the 16 components, yet a ridge model on them predicts the first five PCs of the embedding alone with R² 0.80–0.95 on this library. grover_pca may act as a well-conditioned 2D-descriptor space rather than as learned chemistry. The question is undecided, because neither the embedding columns alone nor the descriptor columns alone reproduce the nearest-neighbour structure of finding 13.

H4 · A favourable reduction of this library open

The lead is concentrated on 21 screened catalysts, is absent on held-out scaffolds and changes with the number of retained components. It may come from how 16 components happen to arrange these 22 catalysts rather than from GROVER itself. This hypothesis predicts that the lead shrinks as new scaffolds are measured.
