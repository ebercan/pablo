Catalyst, solvent, base and condition featurizations for the NHC cross-acyloin condensation

NHC-catalysed cross-acyloin condensation · 69 experiments · 161 catalysts, 14 solvents, 8 bases · 27 catalyst, 5 solvent and 4 base descriptor sets · 23 PCA-reduced catalyst sets

Background

We are optimising the yield of an NHC-catalysed cross-acyloin condensation of nonanal with formaldehyde by batch Bayesian optimisation. Each experiment is a point in a mixed space: four continuous reaction variables (temperature, formaldehyde ratio, catalyst loading and time) and three discrete choices, of catalyst, solvent and base.

A Gaussian process compares two discrete choices only through the vectors that represent them, so the kernel's notion of how similar two catalysts are is the similarity of their descriptor vectors. What a descriptor set measures, and what it cannot see, therefore bounds what the model can learn from 69 experiments. The featurization and kernel scan compares 27 catalyst descriptor sets, 5 solvent sets and 4 base sets. For each we give what it measures, how it is computed, how wide it is, the literature behind it, and the properties of its values that change how a result computed on it should be read. The width of a set is its number of columns.

Locating the carbene centre

Every descriptor computed at the reactive carbon depends on identifying that atom. It is found as a two-coordinate carbon flanked by ring heteroatoms, with the threshold set at one heteroatom rather than two so that cyclic (alkyl)(amino)carbenes and mesoionic centres are matched as well as ordinary N-heterocyclic carbenes. Ring-based identification alone is not enough for this library: C83 is an acyclic diaminocarbene, a tetraisopropyl formamidinium salt whose free carbene contains no ring, and it is classified under the residual ring category. Once identified, the carbene index is stored with the geometry and reused, so every descriptor measures the same atom. C78 carries two carbene centres; one is measured, so its carbene-localised columns describe half the molecule.

ecfp4. Each atom receives an initial identifier from its element and immediate properties. That identifier is hashed together with those of its neighbours, and the process is repeated. Collecting the identifiers produced at every iteration, rather than only the final ones, separates this from the classical Morgan algorithm, and is why the fingerprint reports substructures at every radius up to the limit. Two iterations give a circular environment of radius 2, which by the diameter convention of the name is ECFP4. The fingerprint is stored as counts rather than presence bits, each position holding how many times its environment occurs. Counts make the MinMax kernel meaningful, MinMax on counts being the multiset generalisation of Tanimoto on bits, and this is one of two catalyst sets for which those ratio kernels are chemically appropriate.

map4. MAP4 merges the substructure and atom-pair fingerprint families. For every pair of atoms it writes the circular substructures at radius 1 and radius 2 around both atoms and combines each such pair with the number of bonds separating the two central atoms. The resulting atom-pair molecular shingles carry both local chemical identity and long-range topological separation. They are hashed, MinHashed and stored as a folded 2,048-bit vector. The method was introduced because substructure fingerprints work well on small molecules and atom-pair fingerprints on large ones, with neither performing well on both.

map4 cannot see a halide counterion

Four pairs of catalysts receive an identical map4 vector. C17 and C28 are the same molecule. The other three, C24/C29 (iodide and chloride), C32/C154 (bromide and iodide) and C68/C150 (chloride and bromide), differ only in the halide, which the shingles built around an isolated monatomic anion do not distinguish. ecfp4 separates all three. Conversely, map4 gives ten other pairs of identical molecules different vectors (section 32).

mol2vec. Mol2vec applies the word-embedding construction to chemistry. Morgan substructure identifiers are treated as words and a molecule as a sentence, and an unsupervised model trained on a large compound corpus learns a dense vector for each substructure such that related substructures point in similar directions. A molecule is the sum of its substructure vectors, normalised to unit length. This removes the two structural weaknesses of a folded fingerprint, extreme sparseness and hash collisions, at the cost of any direct interpretation of a single dimension. Substructures absent from the pretraining corpus fall back to one generic vector, and the azolium salts in this library contain substructures well outside a typical drug-like corpus.

mordred. Mordred computes a large catalogue of molecular descriptors: constitutional counts, topological and connectivity indices, autocorrelation functions, estimated charges and polarisabilities, ring and aromaticity measures. Restricted to descriptors that need no three-dimensional structure, it returns 1,613 values per molecule. The raw output is noisy and heavily collinear, so one fixed filter runs over the whole library. A descriptor is discarded if it is undefined or non-finite for any catalyst, if it takes the same value for every catalyst, or if its variance after standardisation is negligible. Where two descriptors correlate above 0.95 in absolute value, only the more variable one is kept, and the survivors are standardised across the library. Four hundred and twenty-seven survive. Every surviving column has a name and a definition, so this set sits between the opaque fingerprints and the quantum-chemical tables in interpretability.

soap. SOAP describes the environment of an atom as a smooth density built from a Gaussian on each neighbouring atom, expands that density on a radial and spherical-harmonic basis, and forms the rotationally invariant power spectrum from the expansion coefficients. The construction is invariant to rotation, reflection, translation and permutation of identical atoms, and varies smoothly as atoms move, which makes it a usable geometric descriptor rather than only a similarity measure. Averaging the coefficients over all atoms before forming the power spectrum gives one vector per molecule. The width follows from the basis and the elements present: nine elements occur (H, C, N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I), and with six radial and four angular functions including cross-element terms this gives 7,425 components, 1.5 times the next widest set. It is the only classical set computed from three-dimensional structure, and it uses the same GFN2-xTB free-carbene geometries as the interpretable sets (section 17).

5. MolFormer-XL

A SMILES string is a lossless serialisation of a molecular graph, so reading it as text discards nothing in principle, and text is the modality where scaling recipes are best understood. The obstacle is cost: standard self-attention is quadratic in sequence length, and 1.1 billion sequences lie far beyond what a BERT-style pretraining run absorbs directly. MolFormer-XL is as much an efficiency result as a chemistry one.

Two substitutions make the scale reachable. Linear attention replaces the softmax kernel with a factorisable feature map, making the cost of an attention layer linear in sequence length. Rotary position embeddings encode position by rotating the query and key vectors through an angle proportional to their index, so the attention logit depends on relative rather than absolute position. The second choice matters for SMILES, because one molecule has many valid strings and a representation keyed on absolute character offsets would treat them as unrelated inputs.

Chemistry encoded

The model receives no coordinates, no bond graph and no conformer. Two bonded atoms can sit many characters apart in the string because of ring-closure digits and branch parentheses. The paper's attention analysis nevertheless shows the learned attention concentrating on atom pairs that are close in three dimensions, including pairs distant in the string. The representation is best read as an implicit statistical model of structure learned from serialisation regularities: strong on the substructure grammar of drug-like chemistry, with no mechanism for stereochemistry beyond what the SMILES spells out.

The released checkpoint is not the model the paper evaluates

The headline model trained on all 1.1 billion molecules was not released. The 10% subset checkpoint, the only public variant and the one loaded here, is trained on a 10 percent subset of both corpora. The paper's benchmark results belong to the unreleased model and do not transfer to molformer_xl_precat or molformer_xl_carbene.

Because the model reads nothing but the string, the pre-catalyst and free-carbene variants differ in the input and in nothing else, which makes this pair the cleanest test of whether decoupling the form from the structure helps. The padding dependence of the pooled vector (section 32) adds noise of its own to both variants.

6. ChemBERTa-2

ChemBERTa-2 leaves the RoBERTa architecture essentially untouched and studies the question underneath it: for molecules, what should the pretraining objective be, and how much data does it need. Labelled molecular data is scarce and unlabelled SMILES are effectively free; the first ChemBERTa established that the transfer recipe works, but not which self-supervised objective gives a representation worth transferring.

The two candidates behave differently. Masked language modelling hides tokens and predicts them, which on SMILES is close to trivial because a handful of atoms dominate the vocabulary. Multi-task regression instead predicts a vector of roughly 200 cheaply computed RDKit descriptors for the whole molecule, a denser and more chemically directed target. Across the sweep, multi-task regression gives the better downstream representation on most MoleculeNet tasks, and gains from more pretraining data do not scale smoothly, which the authors read as evidence that molecular pretraining has not yet found its scaling law.

Chemistry encoded

Because the multi-task target is a descriptor vector, an MTR-pretrained ChemBERTa is trained to be a compressed calculator of what a classical descriptor set returns: molecular weight, topological polar surface area, logP, ring and rotatable-bond counts and the rest of the RDKit 2D block. Its embedding should correlate with mordred, which is computed from the same family of quantities, and a head-to-head between the two compares two routes to similar information.

7. CLAMP

Activity-prediction models must be retrained or fine-tuned for every new target, which is where labels are scarcest. Scientific language models can in principle be prompted about a new task, but their predictive quality on activity is poor. CLAMP keeps the predictive machinery numeric while making the task specifiable in language: it learns a joint space in which an assay description and the molecules active in that assay lie close together, so a new assay can be scored zero-shot from its text.

The molecule tower takes a concatenation of Morgan count and RDKit count fingerprints through a multilayer perceptron. The assay tower embeds the assay's textual description with a language encoder. Training uses a contrastive loss over (molecule, assay) activity records, so both encoders are shaped by the pairing rather than by a structural objective.

CLAMP is a re-mixed fingerprint

The read-out is an 8,192-bit combined Morgan and RDKit count fingerprint passed through the molecule tower, giving a 768-dimensional embedding. CLAMP performs no message passing, no attention over atoms and no geometry; its entire view of a molecule is the fingerprint it is handed. In representation space clamp sits closer to ecfp4 than to any graph or 3D model, and what it adds over the bare fingerprint is a supervision signal drawn from bioassay text.

Chemistry encoded

Substructure presence, filtered through what predicts biological activity. Nothing in CLAMP's training relates to organocatalytic yield, carbene nucleophilicity or steric bulk at a reactive centre. It is in the scan as a well-defined point in representation space, a learned re-mixing of a count fingerprint under a biological prior, not because its pretraining domain matches the reaction.

8. Graphormer

Self-attention computes a similarity between every pair of tokens but is permutation-invariant and blind to position. In language this is repaired with positional encodings that exploit the natural order of words. A molecule has no such order: its atoms live on a graph, position means location in the bonding topology, and two atoms three bonds apart can be far apart in any atom numbering. Graphormer asks what the minimal set of structural signals is that a pure transformer needs, and answers with three encodings, two of which modify the attention score and one the input features.

Figure 1 · Graphormer's three structural encodings

Node degree is added to the atom features at the input. Shortest-path distance and bond-path edge information are added as bias terms to the attention logit, so graph structure reaches the model without changing the transformer block.

Centrality encoding. Self-attention scores nodes by feature similarity, so two atoms with similar local chemistry are indistinguishable to it even when one is a hub and the other a leaf. Graphormer adds a learnable vector indexed by a node's degree to its input features, hi(0) = xi + zdeg(i), which separates a quaternary carbon from a terminal methyl before any message has moved.

Spatial encoding. For every ordered pair the model computes the shortest-path distance on the graph and looks up one learnable scalar, shared across layers, which is added to the attention logit.

Aij = (hiWQ)(hjWK)T / √d + bφ(i,j)

Because the bias is a free function of hop distance, the model can learn to attend strongly to near neighbours and weakly to distant atoms, recovering the locality of a message-passing network, or do the opposite where the chemistry demands it. Every atom still sees every other atom in a single layer; the bias only reports how far away each one is.

Edge encoding. Bonds carry bond order, conjugation and aromaticity. For each pair, Graphormer takes the shortest path between the two atoms, dots each bond's feature vector with a position-specific learnable vector, averages along the path, and adds the scalar to the attention logit as well, so attention between two atoms is modulated by the kind of bonds between them.

A graph-level vector comes from a virtual node connected to every atom, the graph analogue of BERT's classification token, whose final representation is the molecule embedding. A distinct spatial-encoding value marks the virtual connections, so a connection through the supernode is not confused with a real one-bond distance. The authors show that with suitable weights the spatial and edge encodings reproduce the aggregate-and-combine step of GIN, GCN and GraphSAGE, and that the global receptive field lets Graphormer distinguish graphs the 1-Weisfeiler-Lehman test cannot, while avoiding the over-smoothing that limits deep message-passing stacks.

Chemistry encoded

Graphormer's structural priors are purely topological: connectivity through degree, graph distance through shortest paths, and bond type along those paths. It has no representation of geometry, conformers or stereochemistry, so two stereoisomers with the same bond graph map to the same vector. It captures the substructure grammar of molecules well: rings, branches, conjugation patterns, and the difference between a buried and an exposed atom.

9. Transformer-M

Most molecular models commit to one data format. A 2D model cannot use a conformer when one is available, and a 3D model is unusable when only a SMILES string is. The two views describe the same atoms and differ only in whether structure is given through bonds or through Euclidean distances. Transformer-M builds two structural channels on one shared transformer and activates whichever the input provides, which adding structure as bias terms makes natural.

Figure 2 · Two structural channels feeding one backbone

The 2D channel reuses Graphormer's degree, shortest-path and edge encodings. The 3D channel encodes pairwise Euclidean distances through a Gaussian-basis kernel. Each is activated only when that modality is present, and all backbone weights are shared.

The 2D channel is Graphormer: degree encoding added to atom features, shortest-path and bond-path encodings added as attention biases. The 3D channel encodes the only rotation- and translation-invariant quantity available from coordinates, the pairwise distances. Each distance ‖ri − rj‖ passes through a set of Gaussian basis kernels, a soft one-hot over distance ranges with learnable centre and width per atom-pair type, and is projected to a scalar bias per pair. For each atom the 3D encodings to all others are summed and added to its input features.

Why a Gaussian basis for distance

A raw distance is a single scalar and hard for a network to use non-linearly. Expanding it onto K Gaussian bumps exp(−½((d − μk)/σk)²) turns it into a smooth localised feature vector, so the model can respond differently to 1.4 Å, an aromatic C–C distance, and 2.8 Å without assuming the response is linear in distance. Learnable centres and widths indexed by atom-pair type let the model resolve distance where the chemistry varies.

Apart from the two structural encoders, all backbone weights are shared across modalities. Training mixes batches: a 2D-only batch activates the 2D channel, a 3D-only batch the 3D channel, a paired batch both. 2D and 3D data may therefore come from different databases, with no need for paired examples. The model is trained on PCQM4Mv2 to predict the DFT HOMO-LUMO gap, with an added 3D denoising task on the 3D data.

Chemistry encoded

Through the 2D channel Transformer-M inherits Graphormer's topological grammar. Through the 3D channel it reads genuine geometry: bond lengths, through-space contacts, and the spatial arrangement that sets steric bulk and many quantum properties. Because the channels share a backbone, 3D knowledge acquired in pretraining is partly retained when the model is later given only a graph, the idea 3D Infomax is built on.

10. SCAGE

Two problems in structure-activity modelling motivate SCAGE. Activity cliffs are pairs of nearly identical molecules with sharply different activity, precisely where structure-blind models fail. Interpretability matters because a prediction is more useful when the model can point at the functional group responsible. Its design serves both while folding in conformational information without making the backbone a full 3D network.

Figure 3 · SCAGE: distance-masked attention and four pretraining tasks

Left: attention is masked by interatomic distance at several scales, so atoms outside a scale's radius are blocked. Right: four objectives are optimised together, with an adaptive controller reweighting their losses each iteration.

The backbone is a graph transformer over the 2D graph. The distinctive component is multiscale conformational learning: SCAGE generates a conformer with the Merck molecular force field, takes the lowest-energy geometry, derives interatomic-distance thresholds from it and uses them to mask the attention scores. At one scale an atom attends only to its nearest neighbours by distance, at another to a wider shell, and stacking the scales gives a data-driven receptive field rather than a hand-designed cutoff.

Pretraining optimises four objectives together, spanning structure to function: reproducing the Morgan fingerprint, predicting per-atom functional-group annotations derived from motif databases, recovering the 2D shortest-path distance matrix, and predicting bond angles from the MMFF geometry. Four losses on different scales rarely descend in step, so a controller reweights each loss every iteration from its recent rate of decline and its value range, in place of hand-tuned weights.

Chemistry encoded

The functional-group task forces atom representations to carry chemical role, distinguishing a carbon in an ester from a nitrogen in an amide. The bond-angle and distance-masking tasks add conformational geometry, and the fingerprint task anchors a global substructure signature. The functional-group annotation supports attribution of a prediction to substructures and underlies the model's results on activity-cliff benchmarks, where the discriminating change is usually one functional group.

Geometry provenance

SCAGE's 3D signal comes from a single lowest-energy MMFF conformer: a force-field estimate rather than an ensemble or a quantum-chemical geometry. It does not represent conformational flexibility, and the 3D signal is only as good as MMFF is on this chemistry. SCAGE is also the most complex of the eleven models, combining four pretraining tasks, adaptive loss weighting and multiscale masking.

11. GROVER

Two obstacles limit supervised graph networks on molecules: labels are scarce because each is a wet-lab measurement, and models generalise poorly to new chemical space. Self-supervision is the remedy, but NLP recipes transfer badly. Masked-atom prediction is weak because the atom vocabulary is small and dominated by carbon, nitrogen and oxygen. Graph-level pretraining on supervised property labels risks negative transfer, degrading downstream tasks whose objectives conflict. GROVER contributes two self-supervised tasks that avoid both, and an architecture expressive enough to use them.

Figure 4 · The GROVER block and its two self-supervised tasks

A dynamic message-passing network supplies the queries, keys and values for global attention, and a long-range residual counters over-smoothing. Pretraining predicts contextual subgraph properties at the node level and functional-group motifs at the graph level.

An attention block needs vectors as queries, keys and values. GROVER produces them with a message-passing network that reads the local graph and emits per-node and per-edge embeddings, which become the Q, K and V of multi-head attention over the fully connected graph. The message-passing network captures local subgraph structure and the transformer captures long-range relations on top.

Dynamic message passing and the long-range residual

The number of message-passing hops sets a network's receptive field, and a fixed choice rarely suits every molecule. GROVER draws the hop count for a layer from a distribution every epoch, giving each node a random receptive field over training, which acts as a structural regulariser. It also replaces the transformer's many short residual connections with one long-range residual from the input to the final layers, which eases gradient flow and counters over-smoothing in the deep stack.

Contextual property prediction works at node and edge level. Rather than predict a masked atom's bare element, GROVER extracts the k-hop neighbourhood, counts neighbour-atom and bond-type pairs, and serialises them into a key such as C_N-DOUBLE1_O-SINGLE1. Predicting that key from the atom embedding forces the representation to encode the chemical surroundings rather than the element alone. Graph-level motif prediction uses recurrent subgraphs, typically functional groups detected with RDKit, as a multi-label target on the molecule embedding. Because the labels come from a pattern matcher rather than from experiment, the task scales to any unlabelled molecule without the negative-transfer risk.

Chemistry encoded

GROVER is two-dimensional throughout, but its self-supervision is chemically pointed. The contextual task teaches local bonding environments and the motif task teaches functional-group awareness, pushing the representation toward the way chemists describe molecules, as assemblies of recognisable groups in characteristic neighbourhoods.

12. MiniMol

MiniMol argues against scale for molecular models. A graph already respects the molecule's symmetries, permutation invariance and freedom from SMILES ambiguity, so a model with those symmetries built in needs far less capacity. The stated reference point is ULTRA, a 177,000-parameter knowledge-graph model that outperformed much larger baselines by respecting graph symmetry. The target is a model cheap enough to run as a routine fingerprint generator.

The backbone is a message-passing network, by default a graph isomorphism network with edge features, chosen because that family matches the 1-Weisfeiler-Lehman test in expressiveness and the edge-feature variant uses bond information. Sixteen layers bring the total near 10 million parameters. Each atom's input concatenates RDKit atom features with Laplacian eigenvectors and eigenvalues, a graph analogue of Fourier positions, and random-walk return probabilities. A global node connected to every atom routes information between distant parts of the graph and serves as the graph-level read-out.

Figure 5 · MiniMol as a fingerprint extractor

Rich node features, combining RDKit atom features with Laplacian and random-walk positional encodings, feed a 16-layer message-passing stack. Node embeddings are pooled into a fingerprint that downstream tasks consume through a small MLP.

Pretraining uses the Graphium LargeMix dataset: about 6 million molecules and 526 million labels over about 3,300 sparsely defined graph- and node-level tasks, blending quantum-chemistry targets with biological assays and transcriptomic signatures. The loss is a weighted sum over these objectives, with a scaling constant that stops the largest dataset from dominating. Training on quantum and biological signals together is deliberate: the model learns physics it can lend to biology, where labels are scarcer.

Fingerprint extraction rather than fine-tuning

MiniMol is designed to be used as chemists use ECFP. A fixed embedding ψ = Σi hifinal is computed once per molecule and a cheap task head is fitted on top. Embeddings are cached and reused across tasks and sweeps, the downstream model has few parameters and overfits less on small datasets, and the foundation model is never touched. This is the frozen-featurizer pattern under which every model in the scan is used.

Chemistry encoded

MiniMol is graph-based with no coordinates, but the Laplacian and random-walk features give it a strong sense of graph shape, of which atoms are central and how substructures connect, and the quantum tasks in pretraining push it to encode electronic and energetic properties implicitly. Its representation is broad rather than specialised: it has seen molecules through about 3,300 property lenses.

13. Uni-Mol and Uni-Mol2

Uni-Mol is the only model here for which 3D is the primary input. Molecular properties are largely set by three-dimensional structure, yet most representation models start from strings or graphs and treat geometry as auxiliary, which leaves them unable to take 3D positions as input or produce 3D outputs such as binding poses. Uni-Mol is a backbone that is 3D end to end while remaining cheap enough to pretrain at scale.

Atoms are fully connected, so long-range interactions are captured directly, and two problems have to be solved: feeding continuous coordinates into an order-blind transformer, and doing so invariantly to rotation and translation. The answer keeps two coupled representations. The atom representation starts from atom-type embeddings, one token per atom plus a classification token at the geometric centre. The pair representation starts from the pairwise Euclidean distances passed through a pair-type-aware Gaussian kernel, which is invariant to global rotation and translation because it is built from distances.

Figure 6 · Uni-Mol's coupled atom and pair representations

Pairwise distances pass through a Gaussian kernel to initialise the pair representation, which biases the attention logits of an SE(3)-invariant transformer. A lightweight equivariant head predicts coordinate displacements. Pretraining corrupts atomic positions and recovers them.

The two representations exchange information every layer. The query-key products update the pair representation, and the pair representation is added as a bias inside the attention logit, the Graphormer mechanism carrying genuine geometry. The geometry shapes the atom embeddings and the atom embeddings refine the geometry.

Invariant body, equivariant head

An invariant representation does not change when the molecule is rotated or translated, which suits scalar predictions such as energy. An equivariant output rotates with the input, which is required to predict coordinates. Uni-Mol keeps the heavy backbone invariant and attaches a light SE(3)-equivariant head at the end: it predicts each atom's displacement as a sum over pairs of (ri − rj), an equivariant direction, scaled by a learned function of the invariant pair representation. Valid 3D geometry is produced without the cost of an equivariant backbone.

A continuous coordinate cannot be masked like a token, so pretraining adds roughly ±1 Å of uniform noise to each atom and trains the model to undo it through two heads, one predicting the clean positions and one the clean pairwise distances; some atom types are masked and predicted as well. The conformations come from RDKit: 19 million molecules with about 11 ETKDG and MMFF conformers each, roughly 209 million structures. Uni-Mol2 keeps the architecture and studies how it scales, releasing checkpoints from 84 million to 1.1 billion parameters; the 1.1 billion checkpoint is the one used here.

Chemistry encoded

Uni-Mol encodes geometry directly: bond lengths, angles, through-space distances and the spatial arrangement of atoms, which is why it performs well on geometry-dominated properties such as solvation free energy and on 3D tasks such as pose prediction. It needs a conformer, and its output is bounded by the quality of that geometry. A single ETKDG and MMFF conformer is a fast approximation rather than an ensemble, and for flexible molecules the choice of conformer moves the embedding, so conformer generation is part of the representation (section 32).

14. 3D Infomax

Conformer generation is expensive. Accurate methods such as metadynamics or CREST take hours per drug-like molecule, and even fast machine-learned generators are too slow for virtual screens of millions of compounds, so at inference usually only the 2D graph is available. 3D Infomax trains a 2D network to produce 3D-informed representations from a 2D input by borrowing geometry during a one-time pretraining phase; the authors report a mean MAE reduction of about 22 percent on QM9.

Pretraining runs two networks side by side. A 2D network, the one retained, embeds the molecular graph; a 3D network embeds the molecule's conformers. The objective maximises the mutual information between the two embeddings of the same molecule through a contrastive loss, pulling representations of the same molecule together and pushing those of different molecules in the batch apart.

Figure 7 · Distillation of geometry by mutual information

During pretraining the 2D network is required to agree with a 3D network on the same molecule. Afterwards the 3D network is discarded and the 2D network carries implicit geometry into 2D-only inference.

Contrastive distillation

The 2D network is a student and the 3D network a teacher. The only way the student scores high mutual information with the teacher is to predict, from topology alone, what the teacher sees in the geometry, which requires internalising the regularities that link a bond graph to its likely shape. After training only the student is kept, so geometry costs nothing at inference. Unlike augmentation-based self-supervision, which perturbs the graph and assumes the property is unchanged, the supervisory signal is real physical structure.

The 3D encoder must produce an SE(3)-invariant vector, so it works from pairwise distances, which also makes it reflection-invariant: it cannot distinguish enantiomers. Distances are expanded with sine and cosine Fourier features, which help resolve small differences between bond lengths, and a message-passing network runs on the fully connected graph with those distances as edge features. Because a molecule often has several low-energy conformers, the loss treats all conformers of one molecule as mutual positives.

Chemistry encoded

The pretrained 2D network carries implicit three-dimensional information, a learned prior over how graphs fold into space, rather than coordinates. The gains are largest on quantum-mechanical properties, those most determined by geometry, and the authors report no negative transfer and good generalisation even where pretraining and fine-tuning molecules differ markedly in size.

15. Frad

Frad is a pretraining objective rather than an architecture, and rests on an equivalence between denoising and force-field learning. Equilibrium conformers sit at energy minima. Modelling their distribution as a Gaussian centred on each equilibrium makes the score of that distribution, ∇x̃ log p(x̃), equal to −∇x̃E(x̃), the force on each atom. Denoising score matching shows that a network trained to predict the noise added to a structure learns that score up to a constant, so adding Gaussian noise to a relaxed geometry and predicting it back teaches the forces implicitly. The authors report state-of-the-art results on 9 of 12 QM9 targets and 7 of 8 MD17 molecules.

Two failure modes of plain coordinate denoising

Low sampling coverage. To avoid absurd structures such as distorted aromatic rings, coordinate noise must be small, so the model only sees geometries close to equilibrium and learns the force field nowhere else. Isotropic force field. Plain coordinate noise has the same variance in every direction, which assumes the energy rises equally steeply along every coordinate. Real molecules are anisotropic: rings and double bonds are stiff while rotatable single bonds are soft.

Frad uses hybrid noise: a large Gaussian perturbation of the dihedral angles of rotatable bonds, applied with RDKit so that it never produces an invalid structure, then a small Gaussian perturbation of all coordinates. The noise distribution is wide along flexible torsions and narrow on rigid parts, which both covers more low-energy structures and matches the shape of the energy basin.

Figure 8 · Chemistry-aware noise in Frad

Large noise on rotatable dihedrals combined with small coordinate noise produces an anisotropic energy basin, stiff along rings and soft along torsions. Frad denoises only the coordinate fraction, which preserves the equivalence to force-field learning.

Hybrid noise breaks the original equivalence, because the covariance now depends on the input conformation. Fractional denoising restores it: the full hybrid noise is added, but the model predicts back only the coordinate fraction. The authors prove that this learns the anisotropic force field exactly while keeping the coverage and validity benefits. Frad wraps a 3D backbone, here the equivariant TorchMD-NET, and is used both as a pretraining task on a large conformer set and as an auxiliary task during fine-tuning.

Chemistry encoded

Frad builds in the central fact of molecular flexibility: torsions are soft while bonds, angles and rings are stiff. Perturbing exactly the rotatable bonds respects chemical constraints by construction, and the learned anisotropic force field captures how energy varies around a conformer, the basis for energies, forces and geometry-sensitive properties. Like Uni-Mol, it depends on the input geometry.

17. Geometries

All four tables, and soap, are computed on one set of 161 optimised free-carbene geometries. Each pre-catalyst is converted to its free carbene, given a starting structure by distance geometry with experimentally derived torsion preferences (ETKDGv3 [11]), and relaxed with MMFF94 [12]. The structure is then optimised with GFN2-xTB [7], a self-consistent semiempirical tight-binding method with multipole electrostatics and density-dependent dispersion, using the ALPB implicit solvation model [8] parameterised for toluene to match the reaction medium. Every later single-point and adduct calculation uses the same method and solvent, so no descriptor mixes solvation treatments.

A doubly strapped cyclophane cannot be embedded by the torsion-driven method and is embedded by plain distance geometry instead. All 161 catalysts reached a force-field-relaxed starting structure, and none needed a preliminary low-level relaxation before the main optimisation.

18. Substituent bulk

Sterimol parameters [9] describe a substituent by three lengths: L, its extent along the bond to the parent atom, and B1 and B5, its minimum and maximum widths perpendicular to that bond. They are the standard steric descriptors of physical organic chemistry; the conformer-aware treatment is that of Brethómé and co-workers [10].

For interpretable_steric, every ring atom other than the carbene carbon is assigned by element to a nitrogen block or a carbon block, and the substituent it carries contributes one set of Sterimol values to that block. Each block is reduced to five numbers that do not depend on the order of the substituents: how many there are, the largest L, the sum of L, the largest B5 and the smallest B1. An empty block contributes zeros, and the two blocks give ten columns. The largest L and B5 report the most demanding substituent, the sum of L reports total encumbrance, and the smallest B1 reports how thin the least demanding substituent is, which decides whether a substrate can approach at all. The pair of block sizes also encodes topology: two nitrogen and two carbon substituents mark an imidazol(in)ylidene, one and two a thiazolylidene, two and one a ring-fused triazolinylidene.

Hydrogen substituents use literature values

The Sterimol code perceives bonds by interatomic distance. For a single-hydrogen substituent that criterion reaches past the hydrogen to ring atoms beyond it and returns inflated values for what is physically the smallest possible substituent, which would ruin the "smallest B1" column. The canonical Verloop values for hydrogen, L = 2.06, B1 = 1.00 and B5 = 1.00 Å, are used instead.

19. Whole-ligand bulk and buried volume

The columns lp_L, lp_B1 and lp_B5 apply the Sterimol measurement to the whole ligand. The lone-pair direction at the carbene is taken opposite the average of the vectors to its ring heteroatoms, pointing out of the open coordination site. A dummy atom is placed 1.5 Å along that direction, where a metal or a bound substrate would sit, and the whole ligand is measured as one substituent on that dummy. lp_L is the depth of the ligand along the approach axis, and lp_B1 and lp_B5 its narrowest and widest extents across it.

Percent buried volume [13] is the fraction of a sphere centred at the binding site that the ligand occupies. A dummy atom is placed 2.0 Å from the carbene along the lone-pair axis, the standard metal-position convention, and the occupied fraction of a 3.5 Å sphere around it is computed with hydrogens included. The sphere is divided into octants and pair-averaged above and below the ring plane into four quadrants, vbur_q1 to vbur_q4. A single buried volume reports how crowded the site is; the quadrants report where the crowding sits, which separates a symmetric catalyst from one with a single large flanking group. interpretable_light compresses them to their mean (vbur_scalar) and the difference between the largest and smallest quadrant (vbur_asymmetry).

The quadrant frame is reproducible but not chirality-aware

The reference direction that fixes the quadrant frame is chosen deterministically, so repeated calculations agree. It is not a chirality canonicalisation: mirror-image structures can exchange quadrant 1 with 2 and quadrant 3 with 4. The library contains no enantiomeric pairs, so no value is affected, but a chiral scope candidate must be checked by hand before its quadrants are compared with anything.

22. Free energy of deprotonation

interpretable_dG differs from interpretable in one column: it carries xtb_deltaG_deprot, the free energy of removing the C2 proton from the azolium at 298.15 K in toluene, in place of the proton binding energy. The two are the same chemistry at different levels of care. The proton binding energy is an electronic energy from a single geometry derived from the carbene. The free energy of deprotonation treats the azolium and the carbene independently, because the two can prefer different conformations.

For each species up to 20 conformers are generated, pruned at 0.5 Å RMSD, optimised with MMFF and kept if within 10 kcal/mol of the best. Each survivor is optimised with GFN2-xTB and its frequencies computed, giving a rigid-rotor harmonic-oscillator free energy. A conformer is kept only if it converged, has no imaginary mode below −50 cm−1, and still carries the expected number of hydrogens on the carbene carbon; the last check is geometric, so a proton that migrated during optimisation is caught. Conformers within 0.1 kcal/mol and 0.1 Å RMSD of each other are merged as one minimum before Boltzmann averaging at 298.15 K, so a minimum reached from several starts is counted once. The free-proton term is omitted: it is the same constant for every catalyst and cancels in any comparison, so the column is comparable across the library but is not an absolute basicity.

All 161 catalysts completed without a quality failure, and values span 98.9 to 152.2 kcal/mol with a median of 131.2.

Why the column replaces the proton binding energy rather than joining it

Across the library the two are near-collinear: Pearson r = −0.945 and Spearman −0.955 over 161 catalysts, negative because one is a protonation energy and the other a deprotonation energy. Carrying both would put two near-duplicate columns in one block, and a comparison against interpretable would ask only whether the free energy adds anything on top of a column it correlates with at 0.95. Replacing makes the two tables differ in exactly one column and tests whether the conformer ensemble and the thermal correction are worth their cost. Only this column is conformer-averaged; the other 29 come from the single optimised geometry, so interpretable_dG is a set with one conformer-aware column, not a conformer-aware set.

23. Structural and size descriptors

The remaining columns carry no quantum chemistry. A four-column indicator gives the pre-catalyst form (salt, free carbene, CO₂ adduct, bicarbonate). A five-column indicator gives the ring type of section 16. One flag records whether the backbone is saturated, which separates imidazolylidenes from imidazolinylidenes and is the main electronic difference within that pair. Molecular weight and heavy-atom count give overall size.

Categorical variables are expanded into indicators rather than integer codes. The kernels measure real-valued distances, so an integer code would place the fourth category three times further from the first than the second is, which means nothing for an unordered variable; indicator columns place every category at the same distance from every other. Formal charge is absent: every pre-catalyst is net-neutral, so the column would be constant.

29. Basic-site sterics and the base fingerprint

For base_steric each base is reduced to the fragment that performs the deprotonation: the anion for the ionic bases, the neutral amine otherwise. A measurement axis runs from a reference atom to the basic atom, nitrogen for the amines, DBU and KHMDS, oxygen for the alkoxides and carbonates. Part V · Properties of the data that affect interpretation

31. The library holds 150 distinct pre-catalysts and 113 distinct carbenes

The 161 entries are not 161 molecules. Eleven pairs are the same pre-catalyst written as two different SMILES strings: C2/C45, C5/C44, C6/C72, C11/C74, C12/C34, C13/C37, C14/C41, C16/C40, C17/C28, C18/C56 and C21/C109. In every pair the first entry is one of the 22 tested catalysts and the second a scope entry, so half of the tested catalysts reappear, unrecognised, among the 139 scope candidates. Removing the mask collapses the library further: the 161 entries reduce to 113 distinct free carbenes, in 26 groups covering 74 entries, the largest of which is seven entries with one carbene. Within a group the members are the same carbene as a different salt, a CO₂ adduct or a bicarbonate, or the same pre-catalyst entered twice.

A descriptor computed on the pre-catalyst should give identical vectors to an identical pair, and a descriptor computed on the free carbene should give identical vectors within a carbene group. Form, molecular weight and heavy-atom count are the exceptions: they describe the entry, and a CO₂ adduct genuinely has a different molecular weight from the salt of the same carbene.

32. Identical molecules receive different vectors in eleven descriptor sets

Duplicate distance

For a descriptor set, the distance between the vectors of two entries that should be identical, divided by the median, over the library, of each catalyst's Euclidean distance to its nearest chemically distinct neighbour.

ratio = ‖xa − xb‖ / mediani minj distinct from i ‖xi − xj‖

A ratio of 0 means the duplicates coincide; a ratio of 1 means the set puts two copies of one molecule as far apart as a typical catalyst is from its closest different catalyst. For pre-catalyst sets the duplicates are the 11 identical pairs and "distinct" means a different pre-catalyst; for free-carbene sets they are the 26 carbene groups and "distinct" means a different carbene.

Every set should give duplicates a ratio of zero. Sixteen sets do, to within 10−6: ecfp4, mordred, mol2vec, clamp, grover, both variants of MiniMol, Graphormer and 3D Infomax, frad_carbene, transformerm_carbene, and the three harmonised interpretable sets (section 33). Eleven do not.

The causes are in the code that produced the vectors. The two MolFormer sets and chembert2 are mean-pooled over the whole padded batch (features/foundation.py takes last_hidden_state.mean(dim=1) with no attention mask), so a molecule's vector depends on the longest SMILES in its batch of 32 or 64; in molformer_xl_carbene, 17 of the 24 carbene groups whose members have the identical input string still receive different vectors. The string models also see a differently written SMILES as a different input. map4 depends on how the SMILES was entered: ten of the identical pairs have a Tanimoto similarity between 0.555 and 0.947 rather than 1. The conformer-based sets draw an independent conformer for every entry, and in unimol C8 and C80, the same carbene, lie 20 times the typical neighbour distance apart.

The spurious distance matters most where it sits: each of the 11 identical pairs joins a tested catalyst to its untested copy. In the seven affected sets the GP treats the copy as a different catalyst at up to the distance of a real neighbour, so its prediction for the copy does not inherit the tested catalyst's measurements in full, and the copy can be proposed as a scope candidate. The pooling dependence also adds noise to every other catalyst in the string-model sets; the duplicates are only where it can be measured.

33. The interpretable sets are harmonised within carbene groups

Each entry was embedded and optimised independently, and six of the 26 carbene groups converged to slightly different local minima. The largest within-group disagreements were 0.81 percentage points in a buried-volume quadrant (vbur_q4, 7 percent of that column's spread across the library), 0.52 kcal/mol in the deprotonation free energy, and 0.053 Å in a Sterimol width.
