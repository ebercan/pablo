run_5: AF-Optimizer Strategy Comparison

Models × batch-schemes × GA-budget tiers — which q=6 decomposition reaches the best qLogNEI batch, and at what wall-time cost.

0. Setup and Grid

Question. A q=6 batch can be optimized as one joint GA call or built up in smaller sub-batches with already-chosen candidates held fixed. This run measures, for two GP surrogates, how the batch decomposition and the GA generation budget affect the final qLogNEI value the mixed optimizer reaches — and what each scheme costs in wall time.

Two GP setups. Both share grover / map / map featurization (grover catalyst, MAP solvent + base) with a Matérn-5/2 base kernel, fit on percent-scale targets (TARGET_PERCENT_SCALE = 100) so qLogNEI values are positive and match the validated REPRODUCE.py recipe.

Within each model, the four schemes reach essentially the same qLogNEI. The scheme-to-scheme and tier-to-tier differences sit inside one rep-to-rep standard deviation (~0.07–0.10 for st_y, ~0.10–0.18 for lcm_ys). 3. Runtime Breakdown — GA vs L-BFGS Refine

The fixed minimal refine (3 restarts / 128 raw samples) keeps L-BFGS subordinate. For lcm_ys the GA share is large at every tier (≈60–90%) because each AF evaluation on the LCM posterior is expensive. For st_y the cheap single-task AF makes the constant-cost refine a larger relative slice at the low tiers (GA ≈16% at full_batch T0), but GA overtakes refine as the budget climbs. In absolute terms the refine is near-constant overhead; the budget knob is GA generations.

4. Reproducibility and Dispersion

Per-cell standard deviations are reported in the §1 tables. Across the grid, st_y batches are reproducible to ~0.07–0.10 qLogNEI units and lcm_ys to ~0.07–0.19. Dispersion does not shrink with GA budget — the spread is set by the rep-to-rep restart randomness of the GA on a multi-modal discrete landscape, not by under-converged optimization. No scheme is systematically tighter than the others once the budget is matched.

5. Discrete-Choice Diversity

Each q=6 batch concentrates on a small number of distinct catalyst×solvent×base sets (≈2–4 unique combos), with the staged/sequential schemes trending slightly lower than full_batch. Diversity does not grow with GA budget: extra generations recycle the same dominant discrete agents rather than discovering new ones, which is consistent with the flat acq ladders in §1.

6. Efficiency: Acq Value vs Wall Time

Plotting tier-median acq against tier-median wall time collapses the comparison: for both models the full_batch curve sits furthest left (cheapest) at matched acq, and moving to staged or sequential schemes — or to higher tiers — buys wall time without buying acquisition value. There is no point on the frontier where a more expensive scheme earns a higher qLogNEI.

7. Conclusions

  1. Batch decomposition does not change the achieved qLogNEI. Full batch, [3,3], [2,2,2] and fully sequential all land within one rep-to-rep std of each other, for both the single-task and the LCM multitask surrogate.
  2. Full-batch is the efficient choice. It reaches the same acq at the lowest wall time; the staged and sequential schemes only add GA-call overhead. Sequential is the most expensive and earns nothing for it.
  3. The GA converges at the lowest tier. Acq value and discrete diversity are flat across the six budget rungs — additional generations are wasted. Tier-0 budgets are sufficient.
  4. Refine is correctly subordinate. With the pinned 3-restart / 128-sample L-BFGS, GA dominates runtime (overwhelmingly so for the costlier LCM model); the continuous refine is near-constant overhead.
  5. Cost is set by the surrogate, not the scheme. The LCM lcm_ys model is several times more expensive per optimization than st_y; if multitask information is not worth that cost, the single-task yield model with full-batch GA at a low tier is the production recommendation.
