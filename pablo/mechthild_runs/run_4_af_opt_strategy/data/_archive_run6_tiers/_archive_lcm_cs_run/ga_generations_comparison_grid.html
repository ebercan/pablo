AF Optimizer GA-Generations Comparison — run_6

0. Optimization Schemes and Setup Details

Model & objective. The identical fitted GP surface as run_5 — the gem grover / physical / structured / KS1 / lcm_cs / type_linear / raw, a rank-2 LCM KroneckerMultiTaskGP on (conversion, selectivity), serialized once (data/prepared_bundle.pt) and loaded by every worker. The BO objective is yield = C × S and the optimizers maximize qLogNEI to produce a batch of q=6 candidates. AF values are joint-batch qLogNEI on log scale: negative, higher (less negative) is better.

What this run changes. run_6 acts on two findings from the run_5 post-mortem. (i) In run_5 the continuous L-BFGS refinement — not the GA — ate 70–85% of wall time because its restarts/raw samples had been scaled up to 57/2900. (ii) The GA picks the discrete catalyst×solvent×base set, which is the part that matters. So run_6 pins the refine to a fixed minimum (3 restarts / 128 raw, all cells) and pours the freed runtime into GA generations. The qLogNEI MC sample count is also raised 128 → 512 to quarter the estimator noise (±0.035) that scattered run_5's discrete choices near the optimum.

Grid. 2 strategies × 3 runtime tiers (~1 h / 1.5 h / 2 h, differing only in ga_n_evals = generations × pop) × 30 reps = 180 optimizations. One worker = one cell (rep, strategy, tier). Batch GA uses pop 256 (~585–1171 generations on the joint q=6 problem); sequential GA uses pop 128 per greedy step (~312–625 generations ×6 steps). The (2,2,2) temperature partition is enforced on the batch; Time is fixed at 1.0.

Methods. Batch GA + refine (optimize_acqf_mixed_GA): a GA jointly optimizes all 6 candidates, then a single L-BFGS-B continuous refinement on the locked discrete choices. Sequential GA (optimize_acqf_mixed_sequential_GA): greedy k=1…6 construction — each step a GA picks the best next candidate conditional on the fixed ones, immediately followed by a continuous refinement of that candidate.

Run health. All 180/180 workers completed; every result_<idx>.json is present. The per-worker .err logs are all identical 192-byte files carrying a single benign linear_operator Cholesky NumericalWarning (jitter 1e-8 added to the diagonal) — no failures, no NaNs, no OOM at --mem=32G.

Batch GA wins the median at 3/3 tiers. Pooled over all tiers, batch median qLogNEI = -2.2264 (mean -2.2636, sd 0.1023) vs sequential median = -2.2965 (mean -2.3175, sd 0.0750). With the refine pinned and the budget poured into generations, the batch GA's joint q=6 optimization edges ahead — consistent with run_5's finding that batch GA caught and passed sequential once it was no longer generation-starved. Notably, adding generations beyond tier 0 does not improve the median AF — both curves are flat to slightly declining (batch -2.2264 pooled, but tier 0 → tier 2 medians drift -2.2127 → -2.2428). Since the tiers are independent reps rather than paired seeds, this ~0.03 drift is within cell-to-cell sampling noise: both GAs are already at their reachable optimum by ~1 h, and extra generations buy nothing here.

The equal-runtime view

Plotting median AF against the measured median wall time is the fair "who wins per second of compute" view. At any given runtime the higher curve is the better spend.

2. Does the continuous-dimension refiner still help with the budget pinned low?

With L-BFGS pinned to 3 restarts / 128 raw samples, the refiner is deliberately starved. Per-rep AF gain from the continuous refinement, by tier (batch) and by greedy step (sequential).

  • Batch GA: refiner improved the AF in 60.0% of all 90 runs; the gains are tiny (the well-converged 512-MC GA leaves almost nothing for a 3-restart refine to recover).
  • Sequential GA: per-step refiner gain is largest at the early, low-dimensional steps and shrinks toward the final q=6 step; total per-run refiner gain averages -0.0653 qLogNEI.
Pinning the refine did not cost AF: it is still essentially free insurance that never hurts the batch AF, and its marginal value has collapsed precisely because the generation-rich GA now does the heavy lifting. This confirms the run_6 hypothesis — runtime is better spent on GA generations than on continuous restarts.

4. Batch reproducibility and dispersion (per tier)

Each generated q=6 batch is snapped back to exact discrete identities by reverse-lookup: every candidate's catalyst / solvent / base embedding block is matched (argmin L2) to the reference libraries in the prepared bundle, with the base slice treated as absent (id −1) for non-salt catalysts. Batches are compared as unordered sets. Dispersion between two batches is the Hungarian-aligned sum of per-candidate Euclidean distances over the optimal 6→6 pairing of their discrete embeddings, averaged over all rep-pairs in a cell.

Even with 512-MC sampling and ~1171-generation GAs, neither strategy converges to a single stable discrete optimum: pooled over all tiers batch GA produced 48 distinct discrete batches vs sequential GA's 46 (out of 90). The sharper MC ranking did not collapse the discrete spread — the gem surface evidently has many near-degenerate q=6 discrete optima. Hungarian dispersion (raw embedding L2; magnitudes are large because the grover catalyst dims are unscaled, so read it relatively) is comparable between the strategies.
