AF Optimizer Strategy Comparison (Batch vs. Sequential)

0. Optimization Schemes and Setup Details

Model & Setup: A single SingleTaskGP surrogate model is fitted and serialized, then evaluated by 150 independent workers to measure the converged AF value and stability. The optimization objective is to maximize the qLogNEI (q-Log Noisy Expected Improvement) acquisition function to generate a batch of q=6 candidates.

Search Space Dimensionality: The total dimensionality of the mixed-variable search space is 25 per candidate. This consists of:

  • Continuous (4 dimensions): Temperature, Catalyst Loading, Formaldehyde Ratio, and Time (Time is fixed to normalized 1.0).
  • Catalyst (16 dimensions): grover features, dimensionality reduced via PCA using the kneedle algorithm.
  • Solvent (3 dimensions): map features.
  • Base (2 dimensions): map features.

Batch Constraint: The candidates are subject to a strict (2, 2, 2) temperature partition, meaning the 6 candidates must be divided into 3 pairs, with each pair sharing the exact same temperature.

Methods Compared: We compare two optimization schemes for this mixed-variable search space:

  • Batch GA + Continuous Refinement (optimize_acqf_mixed_GA): A Genetic Algorithm (pop_size=500, n_evals=20000) jointly optimizes the entire batch of 6 candidates simultaneously. The (2, 2, 2) temperature constraint is handled natively within the GA space: instead of 6 independent temperature variables, the GA optimizes 3 temperature group variables, inherently tying the candidates together. After the GA converges, the discrete choices are locked, and an L-BFGS-B continuous refiner (num_restarts=20, raw_samples=1024) runs on the continuous dimensions to maximize the joint batch acquisition function.
  • Sequential GA + Intermediate Refinement (optimize_acqf_mixed_sequential_GA): The batch is constructed greedily, step-by-step up to k=6. For each step, the GA (pop_size=500, n_evals=20000) finds the best single candidate conditional on the previously fixed candidates. Immediately after this GA step, L-BFGS-B (num_restarts=20, raw_samples=1024) refines the continuous dimensions of that specific candidate. This newly refined candidate is then appended to the pending set for the next step. The (2, 2, 2) batch constraint is dynamically enforced: the target partition is distributed across the steps, and once a temperature group hits its capacity of 2 candidates, its temperature is fixed for the remainder of the greedy construction, progressively shrinking the free continuous search space.

4. Batch Reproducibility and Dispersion

Because the mixed-variable optimization process contains inherent stochasticity (e.g., initial populations, mutation and crossover in the GA), we evaluated the structural stability of the two optimization strategies by analyzing the variability in their generated batches across all 150 independent runs. Specifically, we investigated whether repeated runs converge to the same discrete choices, and how dispersed the converged batches are within the chemical embedding space.

Methodology: Exact Discrete Matching

Each candidate is defined by a 25-dimensional vector: 4 continuous dimensions and 21 discrete embedding dimensions (16 for the Catalyst, 3 for the Solvent, 2 for the Base). Because the discrete choices are represented as continuous PCA/MAP float embeddings within the surrogate model, comparing them via raw floating-point values can introduce false uniqueness due to numerical precision errors.

To ensure perfect matching, we performed a reverse-lookup mapping. We sliced the 21-dimensional continuous representation back into its three constituent parts and computed the L2 (Euclidean) distance against the exact reference libraries of available catalysts, solvents, and bases. By finding the exact argmin distance, we "snapped" the float embeddings back to their absolute original discrete integer indices. This guarantees that two candidates are identified as identical if and only if they utilize the exact same physical molecules.

Furthermore, because the order of the 6 candidates within a q=6 batch is arbitrary and holds no physical meaning, we treated every generated batch as an unordered mathematical set. We sorted the discrete tuples lexicographically to uniquely identify each converged batch.

Unique Discrete Batches

Applying this exact, order-invariant matching across the 150 independent repeats yielded dramatically different convergence behaviors for the two strategies:

  • Batch GA: Converged to 146 unique discrete batches out of 150 runs. The discrete selection process is almost entirely non-reproducible. Only 4 distinct batches were found twice; the remaining 142 runs converged to completely unique combinations of discrete choices. The Batch GA explores a vast space but fails to exploit a consistent global discrete optimum.
  • Sequential GA: Converged to 73 unique discrete batches out of 150 runs. It demonstrates significantly higher reproducibility and stability. The most frequently discovered discrete batch was found exactly 10 times, with the next most frequent batches occurring 7, 7, 6, and 5 times respectively.

Batch Dispersion and the Hungarian Algorithm

Beyond simply counting exact matches, we analyzed the spatial dispersion of these unique batches. When two runs converge to different unique batches, are those batches chemically similar, or are they scattered randomly across the search space?

To calculate the true geometric distance between two unordered batches (Batch A and Batch B), we cannot simply subtract their matrices, as the candidates are not aligned. Instead, we must find the optimal 1-to-1 pairing of the 6 candidates in Batch A to the 6 candidates in Batch B that minimizes the total distance.

To solve this, we employed the Hungarian Assignment Algorithm. For every pair of unique batches, we computed a 6x6 distance matrix containing the pairwise Euclidean distances between their discrete embeddings. The Hungarian algorithm then identifies the optimal bipartite matching (the assignment that minimizes the sum of the Euclidean distances across all 6 pairings). This minimum sum serves as the absolute distance between the two unique batches.

We computed this Hungarian-aligned distance for all possible pairs of unique batches and averaged the results:

Batch GA2.3127
Sequential GA0.8726
Conclusion: Sequential GA is vastly superior in structural stability. Not only does it converge to the exact same discrete choices across independent runs twice as often as Batch GA, but even when it converges to a mathematically distinct batch, that batch is chemically very similar to the others (a dispersion of 0.87). In contrast, the Batch GA scatters its solutions widely across the embedding space (a dispersion of 2.31).
