AF Optimizer Strategy Comparison — Effort-Ladder Grid

0. Optimization Schemes and Setup Details

Model & objective. A single fitted GP surface — the top featurization×kernel-scan gem grover / physical / structured / KS1 / lcm_cs / type_linear / raw, a rank-2 LCM KroneckerMultiTaskGP on (conversion, selectivity) — is serialized once and loaded by every worker. The BO objective is yield = C × S, and the optimizers maximize qLogNEI (q-Log Noisy Expected Improvement, a sample-based GenericMCObjective) to produce a batch of q=6 candidates. AF values below are this joint-batch qLogNEI on log scale, so they are negative and higher (less negative) is better.

Search space (44 dims/candidate). 4 continuous (Formaldehyde ratio, Catalyst loading, Temperature, Time — Time fixed at 1.0), plus the discrete catalyst×solvent×base embedding blocks (cat 18 incl. form_id+is_salt, solvent 12, base 10). The (2,2,2) temperature partition is enforced on the batch.

What changed vs. the single-budget report. Instead of one fixed optimizer budget, each strategy is now swept over its own 10-rung effort ladder (GA evals, GA population, L-BFGS restarts, raw samples scaled together, ≈×√2 per rung). The ladders were intended to be runtime-matched rung-for-rung (sequential budgets divided by an assumed 2.05× cost factor), but the measured cost ratio on this surface came out lower — sequential GA actually runs at only ≈1.4–1.5× batch GA at the same rung (§3), so at equal rung index sequential is still the slower of the two. Each of the 2×10 cells is repeated 100 times → 2000 optimizations. This lets us ask not just "which strategy wins" but "which wins at equal runtime, and how that depends on the budget rung." The honest equal-runtime comparison is the AF-vs-wall-time view in §1.

Methods. Batch GA + refinement (optimize_acqf_mixed_GA): a GA jointly optimizes all 6 candidates (3 tied temperature-group variables for the (2,2,2) constraint), then a single L-BFGS-B continuous refinement on the locked discrete choices. Sequential GA (optimize_acqf_mixed_sequential_GA): greedy k=1…6 construction — each step a GA picks the best next candidate conditional on the fixed ones, immediately followed by an L-BFGS-B continuous refinement of that candidate; temperature groups lock as they fill.

The equal-runtime view

Since the ladders are not exactly runtime-matched in practice (§3), plotting median AF against the actual median wall time is the fair "who wins per second of compute" view — at any given runtime, the higher curve is the better spend. Batch GA sits up-and-left of sequential GA across the whole range: better AF at lower cost.

2. Does the continuous-dimension refiner improve AF, and how much across budgets?

Batch GA

Per-rep AF gain from the single post-GA continuous refinement, averaged within each rung.

  • Batch GA: refiner improved the AF in 100.0% of all 1000 runs; mean gain decreases as the GA budget grows (a well-converged GA leaves less for the refiner to recover).
  • Sequential GA: the per-step refiner gain (right panel) is largest at the early, cheap steps and small at the final q=6 step; total per-run refiner gain averages 0.0291 qLogNEI.
The continuous refiner is essentially free insurance: it never hurts the batch AF and its marginal value shrinks exactly where the GA is already strong (high rungs, late sequential steps).

4. Batch reproducibility and dispersion (per rung)

Each generated q=6 batch is snapped back to exact discrete identities by reverse-lookup: every candidate's catalyst / solvent / base embedding block is matched (argmin L2) to the reference libraries in the prepared bundle, with the base slice treated as absent (id −1) for non-salt catalysts. Batches are compared as unordered sets. Distance between two unique batches is the Hungarian-aligned sum of per-candidate Euclidean distances over the optimal 6→6 pairing of their discrete embeddings.

The strong sequential-stability advantage from the single-budget run does not survive on this surface. At low budget both strategies are almost fully non-reproducible (≈100/100 unique discrete batches); reproducibility improves only modestly with budget, and sequential GA holds just a slight edge at the high rungs (e.g. rung 9: 81 unique vs batch's 90). Pooled across all 10 rungs, batch GA produced 856 distinct discrete batches vs sequential GA's 754 (out of 1000) — a real but small gap, not the ~2× of the original run. Hungarian dispersion (raw embedding L2; magnitudes are large because the grover catalyst dims are unscaled, so read it relatively) is comparable between the two and actually slightly lower for batch GA at the top rungs. Net: on the Kronecker gem surface with ~140-generation GAs, neither strategy converges to a stable discrete optimum, and batch GA's discrete spread is no worse than sequential's.
